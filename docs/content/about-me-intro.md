# About Me — Intro Copy (from Ananya)

ananya banerjee
designer • student • creative

I'm Ananya, a UX designer based in Atlanta, GA with a passion for finding clarity within chaos and complexity. Much like making a collage, my approach to design starts with a collection of seemingly disconnected pieces—people, perspectives, problems, ideas—and asks: what's the bigger picture?

I'm currently an undergrad student at Georgia Tech studying Computational Media (Human-Computer Interaction and Interaction Design) and Industrial Design.

Outside of design and collaging, I have a deep love for fashion, music, and food! You'll probably find me throwing clothes around my room trying to put on the perfect outfit, blasting EDM at all times of the day, or feasting on some combination of spicy ramen, chicken and chips.

## Why collages?

When I was a kid, my dream career changed almost every week—singer, dancer, teacher, scientist, artist... I wanted to do everything. I've always been drawn to seemingly unrelated interests, and as I grew more connected to my creative side, collaging became a way for me to bring all of those pieces together.

I started collaging everything: my bedroom walls, phone and laptop cases, sketchbooks, mood boards, and eventually my design process itself. I'd pull pieces from music, movies, fashion, food, art, and whatever else caught my attention and arrange them until something distinctly me emerged.

I still approach new ideas this way. When I'm faced with something big or messy, my first instinct is to put all the pieces in front of me. Then I look for patterns, make connections, and slowly uncover the idea tying everything together.
And, that's also how I think about design.

Research gives me pieces that don't always seem to fit together at first: people's behaviors, frustrations, needs, perspectives, and the constraints surrounding them. I love figuring out how those smaller details connect, and how they can transform into an experience that feels clear and intentional.

To someone who doesn't know me, a collage I've made might look like a chaotic collection of unrelated things. But look closely, and every piece is there for a reason.

I think good design works the same way: understand the pieces, find the connections, and create something greater than the sum of its parts.
